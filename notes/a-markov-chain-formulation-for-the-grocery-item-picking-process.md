Walmart models grocery picking and substitution as an absorbing Markov chain whose terminal states are customer acceptance, rejection, and no item picked. Transition matrices let analysts estimate final outcomes from intermediate states and evaluate proposed changes such as on-demand substitutions. A second worked example uses hypothetical probabilities to model chilled-item deterioration during delivery and shows acceptance falling from 82.5% to 78% as exposure to ambient conditions increases.
